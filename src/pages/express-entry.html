Express Entry

How Express Entry Works

Express Entry is not itself an immigration program. It is the system the Government of Canada uses to manage applications for three economic programs, selecting skilled workers on their ability to contribute to the Canadian economy.

  • Federal Skilled Worker Program — skilled workers with foreign work experience
  • Federal Skilled Trades Program — qualified tradespeople
  • Canadian Experience Class — those with skilled Canadian work experience

Eligible candidates enter a pool and are given a score under the Comprehensive Ranking System. At each draw, the highest-ranked candidates receive an Invitation to Apply. Provinces and territories can also recruit from the pool through the Provincial Nominee Program to meet local labour needs.

Federal Skilled Worker Program

This program targets skilled workers with foreign work experience who want permanent residence in Canada. Candidates must first meet every minimum requirement. Only then is the application assessed on the selection factors.

Minimum requirements

All three must be met — this is a threshold, not a score:

  • Skilled work experience
  • Language ability
  • Education

Selection factors

Candidates who clear the threshold are then assessed on:

  • Age
  • Education
  • Work experience
  • A valid job offer
  • English and/or French language skills
  • Adaptability — how likely you are to settle successfully

Skilled Work Experience

Experience must fall within one of these National Occupational Classification categories: managerial jobs (skill type 0), professional jobs (skill level A), or technical jobs and skilled trades (skill level B).

You must be able to show that, in your primary occupation, you performed the duties set out in the lead statement of the NOC description — including all the essential duties and most of the main ones.

The experience must be

  • Within the last ten years
  • Paid work — volunteering and unpaid internships do not count
  • In the same NOC as the occupation you are applying under
  • At least one year continuous, or 1,560 hours in total
1,560 hours can be reached in more than one way

Part-time hours can be more or fewer than 15 per week, as long as they add up to 1,560.

Language Ability

You must take an approved test in English or French covering writing, reading, listening and speaking, and reach the minimum in all four abilities for your first official language.

Minimum level required in all four abilities
LanguageMinimum level
EnglishCanadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 7
FrenchNiveaux de compétence linguistique canadienne (NCLC) 7

Additional points for French

Up to 30 additional points are available for strong French, even where French is your second language. Scoring NCLC 7 or higher across all four French abilities earns:

  • 15 points if you scored CLB 4 or lower in English, or took no English test
  • 30 points if you scored CLB 5 or higher across all four English abilities

Language results are valid for two years from the test date, and must still be valid on the day you apply for permanent residence — not the day you enter the pool. This catches people out.

Education

If you studied in Canada, you need a certificate, diploma or degree from a Canadian secondary or post-secondary institution. For foreign education, you need a completed credential plus an Educational Credential Assessment report from an approved agency, confirming your qualification is equivalent to the Canadian standard.

The Comprehensive Ranking System

Meeting the minimum requirements gets you into the pool. The CRS decides whether you are invited out of it. Scores are made up of core points and additional points.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I need a job offer for Express Entry?

No. A valid job offer is one of the selection factors and adds additional CRS points, but it is not a requirement for the Federal Skilled Worker Program. Many candidates are invited without one.

How long are my language test results valid?

Two years from the date of the result. They must still be valid on the day you apply for permanent residence, not merely on the day you entered the pool — so a test taken early in the process can expire at exactly the wrong moment.

Does part-time work count towards the one-year requirement?

Yes. What matters is reaching 1,560 hours in your primary occupation. Fifteen hours a week for 24 months counts the same as 30 hours a week for 12 months. Hours beyond 30 per week in a single job do not accumulate faster.

I only speak a little French. Is it still worth testing?

Possibly. Reaching NCLC 7 across all four French abilities is worth up to 30 additional points even when French is your second language. Whether it is worth the effort depends on how close your score already is to recent draw cut-offs — we will tell you during the assessment.

What happens if my application is refused?

It depends on the reason. Some refusals can be addressed and the application resubmitted; others point to an eligibility problem that a different program would suit better. Where a procedural fairness letter is issued first, there is an opportunity to respond before a decision — and that response matters a great deal.
