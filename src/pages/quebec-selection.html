Quebec Selection

How it works

Selected by Québec, Not Express Entry

Under the Canada–Québec Accord, Québec chooses its own economic immigrants and runs its own points grid — separate from federal Express Entry and the Provincial Nominee Program, which do not select for Québec at all. If you intend to settle in Québec, this is the route your application follows.

Every year Québec receives immigrant workers whose skills match particular labour market needs. To immigrate to Québec you must first be selected by the province and issued a Certificat de sélection du Québec (CSQ) — only after that do you apply to the federal government for permanent residence.

Skilled workers

The Skilled Worker Selection Program

The Programme de sélection des travailleurs qualifiés (PSTQ) is Québec’s main route for skilled workers, whether you are already in Québec or applying from abroad. It replaced the former Programme régulier des travailleurs qualifiés (PRTQ) in November 2024, and covers the Québec job market through four separate streams, each with its own selection criteria for the occupations it targets.

You start by submitting a declaration of interest through Arrima. Your profile is scored against a points grid covering factors such as education, area of training, work experience, age, language ability in French and English, and family situation, then held in a bank of declarations. A high enough score can bring an invitation to apply for permanent selection — and, once that application is approved, your CSQ.

Québec assesses French separately from the rest of Canada. For most PSTQ streams, a minimum spoken (and for some streams, written) French level is a selection condition — not just a scoring factor — with only a narrow exception for exceptional-talent candidates. We confirm the exact level your profile needs at assessment.

Already in Québec

The Québec Experience Program

The Programme de l’expérience québécoise (PEQ) closed in November 2025 and reopened on a temporary basis in July 2026, running through July 2028 while Québec treats the PSTQ as its long-term skilled worker system. It remains a shorter path to a CSQ, but only for candidates who already met its eligibility conditions before the program closed.

Because eligibility is tied to that cutoff and current intake periods, whether PEQ is open to your profile right now depends on your specific work history, graduation date and French level. We check this at assessment before recommending it over the PSTQ.

  • Two eligibility tracks: qualifying Québec work experience, or a qualifying Québec diploma
  • Currently open only to candidates who met PEQ conditions as of the November 2025 cutoff, within defined intake windows

Questions

Québec Selection Questions

Is Québec part of the Provincial Nominee Program?

No. Québec runs its own selection system under the Canada–Québec Accord and does not take part in the Provincial Nominee Program or federal Express Entry. It has its own portal, its own points grid, and its own certificate — the CSQ.

What is Arrima?

Arrima is Québec’s official online immigration services portal. Skilled worker candidates use it to submit a declaration of interest, which is scored and held in a bank of profiles until an invitation is issued.

What is the CSQ, and does it replace my permanent residence application?

No. The Certificat de sélection du Québec confirms the province has selected you. You still need to apply separately to the federal government for permanent residence once you hold it.

Do I need to speak French?

For most PSTQ streams, yes — a minimum French level is a mandatory selection condition, not just a points factor. The exact level depends on your occupation and stream, with a narrow exception for exceptional-talent candidates. We confirm what your profile needs at assessment.

What is the difference between the PSTQ and the PEQ?

The PSTQ is Québec’s general, points-based route and its long-term skilled worker system. The PEQ is a shorter path for people with qualifying Québec work experience or a Québec diploma, but it is currently open only on a temporary basis (2026–2028) to candidates who met its conditions before its November 2025 closure — we assess which route actually fits your situation.

Can I bring my family?

Yes. Your spouse or common-law partner and dependent children can be included in your application, and family composition is itself one of the factors in the points grid.
