Visits, Super Visas and Sponsorship

Family applications are refused more often than most applicants expect, and usually for the same reasons: thin evidence of the relationship, unclear finances, or an officer who is not satisfied the visitor will leave at the end of an authorised stay.

We build these files to answer those questions before they are asked. That is the difference between a visitor visa that is issued and one that comes back with a refusal letter you then have to explain in every future application.

Questions

Visit & Sponsorship Questions

What is the difference between a visitor visa and a super visa?

A visitor visa normally permits a stay of up to six months at a time. A super visa is specifically for parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens and permanent residents, allows much longer stays per entry, and requires proof of private medical insurance and of the inviter's income.

Why are visitor visas refused so often?

Almost always because the officer is not satisfied the applicant will leave at the end of an authorised stay, or that the visit is funded and genuine. Strong ties at home, clear finances and a coherent invitation address that directly — which is what we build the file around.

Who can I sponsor?

A spouse, common-law or conjugal partner, dependent children, and — when the programme is open — parents and grandparents. Other relatives can be sponsored only in narrow circumstances. Each category has different evidence and income requirements.

What is the undertaking?

A binding commitment to financially support the person you sponsor for a set period, which varies by relationship. It continues even if circumstances change — including separation — so it is worth understanding properly before you sign.

Can someone already in Canada apply from inside?

In some cases, yes. Spousal sponsorship in particular can be processed inland or from overseas, and the two routes behave differently on timing, work permits and travel. The right choice depends on your situation.

My parents were refused before. Is it worth trying again?

Frequently, yes — but not by resubmitting the same application. A refusal usually tells you what the officer was not satisfied about. Bring the letter to the assessment and we will tell you honestly whether the gap can be closed.
