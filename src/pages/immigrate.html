Which Permanent Residence Route Fits You?

Canada runs several permanent residence streams at once, and they do not all want the same applicant. Express Entry ranks candidates against each other. Provincial programs target specific occupations and regions. Quebec runs its own selection entirely.

The route that suits a 29-year-old engineer with strong English is rarely the route that suits a 44-year-old tradesperson with a provincial job offer. We assess your full profile and recommend the stream you actually stand the best chance in.

Questions

Permanent Residence Questions

What is the difference between Express Entry and a Provincial Nominee Program?

Express Entry is a federal pool where candidates are ranked against each other and invited by score. A Provincial Nominee Program is a province choosing you directly because your occupation or profile matches what it needs. The two connect: a provincial nomination adds 600 points to an Express Entry profile, which effectively guarantees an invitation.

Can my family come with me?

Yes. A permanent residence application normally includes your spouse or common-law partner and your dependent children. Their details form part of the same application, and your partner's education and language ability can add points to your score.

How long does permanent residence take?

It varies considerably by stream, and published service standards move. Rather than quote a number that may be out of date by the time you apply, we give you a current, written estimate for your specific route at the assessment stage.

Does Quebec work differently?

Yes. Quebec selects its own economic immigrants under its own criteria and its own ministry, separately from the federal system. Mr Badal is a member of the Registre québécois des consultants en immigration, so we can advise on both.

When can I apply for citizenship?

Once you have been physically present in Canada for three years out of the previous five as a permanent resident, along with the other statutory requirements. Time spent in Canada before becoming a PR can count partially towards it.

Do I have to live in the province that nominated me?

You are expected to. A provincial nomination is granted on the basis that you intend to settle and work in that province, and that intention is assessed. It is not a formality.
