Overview

The Final Step

Once you have held permanent resident status for long enough, you may be eligible to apply for Canadian citizenship — the last step in the journey, and the only one that carries a Canadian passport, the right to vote and protection from ever losing your status through a change in immigration law.

IRCC assesses every application against four requirements: physical presence, tax filing, language ability and the citizenship test. We check all four before you file, so nothing comes back later as a surprise.

  • Open to permanent residents who meet the presence and filing requirements
  • Processing times vary; we confirm the current estimate at assessment
  • Minor children can be included on a parent’s application in most cases

Physical presence

1,095 Days in Five Years

You must have been physically present in Canada for at least 1,095 days — the equivalent of three years — during the five years immediately before you apply. The days do not need to be continuous.

  • Time spent in Canada before becoming a permanent resident can count, at half a day per day, up to a maximum of 365 days
  • Time spent outside Canada does not count, with narrow exceptions such as accompanying a Canadian spouse posted abroad
  • IRCC checks travel history against passport stamps, so an accurate day count matters

Keep a travel log as you go. Reconstructing five years of travel dates after the fact is the single most common reason a citizenship application stalls. We help clients track this from the day they land.

Tax filing

Filing for at Least Three Years

If you were required to file Canadian income taxes under the Income Tax Act, you must have done so for at least three of the five years before you apply. IRCC checks this directly with the Canada Revenue Agency.

Language ability

English or French to CLB 4

Applicants aged 18 to 54 must demonstrate basic ability to speak and listen in English or French, to Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) level 4. Applicants outside that age range are exempt.

  • An approved test result, such as IELTS or TEF, is the most common form of proof
  • Evidence can also include completed secondary or post-secondary study in English or French
  • Only speaking and listening are assessed for citizenship — not reading or writing
Citizenship test

Rights, History and Government

Applicants aged 18 to 54 sit a test covering the content of the official study guide, Discover Canada.

  • Canadian history, geography and symbols
  • The rights, responsibilities and privileges of citizenship
  • How Canada’s government and electoral system work

The test is in English or French only. We point clients to the current study guide and confirm which test format applies to their application.

Questions

Citizenship Questions

How many days do I need to have been in Canada?

At least 1,095 days — three years — during the five years immediately before you apply. The days do not need to be continuous, and time as a temporary resident can count toward part of it.

Do I need to file taxes to qualify?

If you were required to file under the Income Tax Act, yes — for at least three of the five years before you apply. IRCC verifies this with the Canada Revenue Agency directly.

Is everyone required to take the citizenship test?

No. Only applicants aged 18 to 54 at the time they apply sit the test and meet the language requirement.

Can my children apply with me?

In most cases, minor children can be included on a parent’s application, provided they are also permanent residents.

What happens after my application is approved?

You attend a citizenship ceremony, take the Oath of Citizenship, and receive your citizenship certificate — after which you can apply for a Canadian passport.
