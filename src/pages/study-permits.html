Studying in Canada

More than 90,000 students come to study in Canada every year, and more still come to learn English or French. Canada welcomes foreign students’ knowledge and skills, and the culture they bring to the classroom.

Education and schools are regulated by the provinces. To study in Canada you may need a study permit or a temporary resident visa, though not everyone requires these documents. Before you can apply for a study permit you must have been accepted at a recognised school, university or college in Canada.

  • Primary and secondary schools teach up to grade 12, regulated by the provincial Ministry of Education
  • Post-secondary institutions include colleges, universities and technical schools
  • Each post-secondary school sets its own application rules and its own required level of English or French

After graduation

Post-Graduation Work Permit

You have 180 days after your school issues your final marks to apply for a post-graduation work permit. To be eligible, your study permit must have been valid at some point during those 180 days.

  • Completed a study programme at a designated learning institution that was at least eight months long
  • Maintained full-time student status during each semester, except the final one, which may be part-time
  • Graduated from a public post-secondary school — a college, trade or technical school, university or CEGEP
  • Or a private post-secondary school in Quebec operating under the same rules as a public one
  • Or a private school in Quebec offering a qualifying programme of 900 hours or longer leading to a DEP or ASP
  • Or a Canadian private school authorised to award degrees under provincial law
During your studies

Co-op Work Permit

International students following a programme at a designated learning institution that requires a co-op internship or work placement may apply for a co-op work permit.

This is a separate permit, distinct from the authorisation to work part-time that is generally included with a Canadian study permit. It may only be used for employment that is necessary to the programme of study — not for an internship or placement at an unrelated organisation that carries no course credit.

Language classes

IELTS and TEF Tuition

Continuous Learning Abroad also runs IELTS and TEF tuition classes.

The International English Language Testing System is a standardised test of English proficiency for non-native speakers, assessing reading, writing, listening and speaking. The Test d’Évaluation de Français, created in 1998 by the Paris Ile-de-France CCI, is the international benchmark for French.

  • TEF Canada is the version used for immigrating to Canada and obtaining Canadian citizenship
  • A separate version is used for immigrating to Quebec
  • Both tests confirm competency in a professional, academic or mobility context

Questions

Study Questions

Do I need to be accepted before I apply for a study permit?

Yes. Before you can apply for a study permit you must have been accepted at a recognised school, university or college in Canada.

How long do I have to apply for a post-graduation work permit?

You have 180 days after your school issues your final marks. Your study permit must have been valid at some point during that window.

How long must my programme be to qualify for a PGWP?

The study programme must have been at least eight months long and completed at a designated learning institution.

Is a co-op work permit the same as working part-time on a study permit?

No. They are separate. A co-op work permit covers employment that is a required part of your programme; it cannot be used for unrelated work that carries no course credit.

Which French test do I need for Canada?

TEF Canada is the version used for immigrating to Canada and obtaining Canadian citizenship. A different version applies for immigrating to Quebec.
