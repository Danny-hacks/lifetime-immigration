Sponsorship

Who You Can Sponsor

If you are a Canadian citizen or a permanent resident of Canada, you can sponsor your spouse, common-law partner, conjugal partner, dependent child (including an adopted child) or another eligible relative — such as a parent or grandparent — to become a permanent resident.

The process begins when you, as a citizen or permanent resident, apply to be a sponsor. There are two different processes, and which one applies depends on who you are sponsoring.

  • One process for a spouse, conjugal or common-law partner and dependent children
  • A separate process for other eligible relatives

Partner & children

Sponsoring a Partner or Dependent Children

You are eligible to sponsor a spouse, a common-law or conjugal partner, or dependent children living outside Canada. The person sponsored has to be a member of the family class.

  • You are 18 years of age or older
  • You are a Canadian citizen or a valid permanent resident of Canada
  • You live in Canada — or, if you are a citizen living abroad, you can show you will live in Canada once your partner or children arrive
  • You agree to an undertaking to provide for the basic requirements of the person sponsored
  • You and the person sponsored sign a mutual agreement confirming your obligations to each other
Parents & grandparents

The Parents and Grandparents Program

Canada’s family class sponsorship program includes a stream dedicated entirely to the parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens and permanent residents. Those approved under it are eligible for permanent residence and may eventually apply for Canadian citizenship.

To sponsor under the Parents and Grandparents Program, the sponsor must meet the following criteria.

  • Be a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, or a person registered under the Canadian Indian Act
  • Be 18 years of age or older on the date of application
  • Be residing in Canada, with proof provided
  • Meet the requisite financial status, known as the minimum necessary income
Other relatives

The Lonely Canadian Category

Also known as the Lonely Canadian Program or the other relative program. Under it, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident can sponsor an adult son, daughter, brother, sister, uncle, nephew or niece to immigrate to Canada. The person sponsored must be a blood relation.

It is designed for Canadian sponsors who have no other family connections in Canada and no other person they could sponsor.

  • The sponsor must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident
  • The sponsor must not have a spouse or common-law partner who is a citizen or permanent resident
  • The sponsor must not have a parent, grandparent or child who is a citizen or permanent resident
Visiting

Visitor Visa and Super Visa

A Canada visitor visa is the document that permits a visit for a limited period, whether for tourism or study, where the stay is less than six months.

The Parent and Grandparent Super Visa permits parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens and permanent residents to visit for much longer periods than an ordinary visitor visa allows.

  • All Super Visa applicants must hold private medical insurance before they land in Canada
  • Lifetime Immigration provides that insurance service for Super Visa holders

Check the current stay length before you rely on it. The rules governing how long a Super Visa holder may remain per entry have been revised since this programme began, and the figure that applied a few years ago is no longer the one in force. We confirm the current position at assessment.

Questions

Sponsorship Questions

Who counts as an eligible relative?

A spouse, common-law partner, conjugal partner or dependent child, including an adopted child. Beyond that, parents and grandparents under the PGP, and in narrow circumstances an adult sibling, niece, nephew, uncle or aunt under the other relative category.

What is the undertaking?

A commitment to provide for the basic requirements of the person you sponsor. You and the person sponsored also sign a mutual agreement confirming your obligations to each other.

Can I sponsor if I am a Canadian citizen living abroad?

For a partner or dependent children, yes — provided you can demonstrate a genuine intention to live in Canada once they arrive. That intention is assessed on evidence.

Does a Super Visa holder need medical insurance?

Yes. All applicants must be covered by private medical insurance before they land in Canada. Lifetime Immigration provides this service for Super Visa holders.

How long can a visitor stay?

An ordinary visitor visa covers a stay of less than six months. A Super Visa is designed for considerably longer stays, and the current permitted length is confirmed at assessment because it has changed over time.
